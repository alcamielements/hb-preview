The red line
Seven centimetres, embroidered, centre back, five below the neckline. Thread matched to the dye lot. It is on every piece we have ever made and it is the only branding on this one.
Care
Cold wash inside out, hang dry. It will fade. That is the dye doing what garment dye does, and it is why the second year looks better than the first.

Sizing, cloth, construction

The Body

One block. Three cloths. Four colours. Every piece we make is cut from this, so once you know your size here you know it everywhere on the site.

Set shoulder, not dropped

An oversized blank hangs off you. A cut one sits on you. The difference is a shoulder seam that lands where your shoulder actually ends, and everybody can feel it even if they cannot name it. It is also the single most expensive decision in the pattern, which is why almost nobody at this price does it.

The three cloths

Pima 240

Combed ring-spun long-staple Peruvian Pima, 240gsm single jersey, garment-dyed and enzyme-softened. Tees and long sleeves, all year.

Pima 450

Loopback Pima fleece, 450gsm, garment-dyed. Hoods, crews and joggers. Heavy enough that it hangs rather than puffs.

Linen 60/40

Sixty per cent European wet-spun linen, forty per cent Pima, 190gsm. Keeps the slub and the dry hand without bagging at the neck the way pure linen jersey does.

The four colours

Noir, Bone, Ash, Oxblood. They do not change between seasons and we do not add to them. Four colours is the reason thirty-two different styles read as one wardrobe instead of a market stall.
